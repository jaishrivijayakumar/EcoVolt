## 2. The Problem Statement

The EV battery waste crisis is one of the fastest-growing environmental challenges accompanying the global transition to electric mobility. The EcoVolt website opens with a data-driven problem framing that establishes urgency around four core issues:

### The Environmental Threat

Lithium-ion batteries used in electric vehicles contain heavy metals — primarily lithium, cobalt, nickel, and manganese — that pose severe risks when improperly discarded. When batteries end up in landfills, these materials leach into groundwater and soil, contaminating ecosystems and threatening public health over long periods.

### Thermal Runaway and Fire Risk

Degraded or damaged lithium-ion cells are chemically unstable and prone to thermal runaway — an uncontrolled, self-sustaining exothermic chain reaction that causes intense fires difficult to suppress with conventional methods. These fires have occurred in landfills and scrapyards globally and represent a growing public safety concern.

### The Scale of the Problem

The website quantifies the crisis with four key statistics that anchor the user's understanding of scope:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| EVs expected on global roads by 2030 | 140 million+ |
| Projected spent lithium battery waste by 2030 | 11 million tonnes |
| EV batteries currently recycled properly worldwide | Less than 5% |
| Average EV battery lifespan before replacement | 8 to 10 years |

These figures collectively illustrate that the problem is not hypothetical — it is imminent and already underway, with the gap between battery production volume and proper recycling infrastructure representing a critical market and environmental opportunity.

## 3. The Solution — EcoVolt

EcoVolt positions itself as the end-to-end infrastructure solution that closes the loop between EV battery end-of-life and material re-entry into the supply chain. The venture addresses three simultaneous gaps in the current ecosystem:

**Logistics Gap:** No organized, certified, doorstep collection infrastructure exists at scale for spent EV batteries in India.

**Processing Gap:** Advanced hydrometallurgical recovery technology capable of extracting 95%+ of usable materials is not widely accessible or deployed for EV batteries at the consumer level.

**Compliance Gap:** Individual EV owners and fleet operators struggle to fulfill CPCB Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) mandates without dedicated service infrastructure.

EcoVolt solves all three in a single, integrated service offering.

## 9. Business Model Context

EcoVolt operates as a B2C and B2B recycling service with revenue generated across multiple streams:

**B2C — Individual EV Owners:** Paid collection and recycling service per battery unit. Revenue from service fees.

**B2B — Fleet Operators and Dealerships:** Volume-based contracts for regular battery collection. Tiered pricing by battery count and frequency.

**B2B — Battery Manufacturers and Importers:** EPR compliance documentation services. Manufacturers are legally obligated under Battery Waste Management Rules 2022 to ensure batteries are properly recycled; EcoVolt provides both the recycling service and certified proof of compliance.

**Material Recovery Revenue:** Recovered lithium, cobalt, nickel, and manganese are sold back into the battery manufacturing supply chain. Cobalt in particular carries high market value, creating a secondary revenue stream from the recycled material itself.

**Corporate ESG Partnerships:** Enterprise agreements with EV OEMs, corporate fleets, and sustainability-focused organizations to manage their entire battery waste portfolio, including Live Impact Dashboard access and branded ESG reporting.

## 10. Regulatory and Compliance Context

EcoVolt is designed to operate within and support compliance with the following Indian regulatory frameworks:

### Battery Waste Management Rules 2022

Notified by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), these rules mandate Extended Producer Responsibility for battery manufacturers and importers. Producers must ensure a defined percentage of batteries sold are collected and recycled, with the percentage target increasing annually. EcoVolt's compliance certificate service directly enables producers to fulfill and document this obligation.

### Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) Guidelines

The CPCB regulates the management of hazardous waste, including lithium-ion batteries, under the Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules. EcoVolt's certified transport and processing operations are designed to meet CPCB standards.

### Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) Framework

EPR places the responsibility of end-of-life product management on the original producer. EcoVolt's corporate-facing service tier specifically helps manufacturers, importers, and brand owners meet their EPR tonnage targets through certified collection and recycling documentation.

## 11. Impact Metrics and Projections

The projected Year 1 impact figures on the website are grounded in the following assumptions:

**10,000+ batteries recycled** — Based on conservative estimates of accessible EV penetration in key urban markets (Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Pune) combined with fleet operator partnerships seeded during the launch phase.

**500 tonnes of materials recovered** — Average EV battery weighs approximately 300–600 kg. A mix of passenger and commercial vehicle batteries at 10,000+ units corresponds to approximately 3,000–5,000 tonnes of total battery mass, with recoverable material yield estimated at 10–15% by weight for key metals.

**2,200 tonnes of CO2 prevented** — Based on lifecycle emissions calculations for lithium, cobalt, and nickel mining and primary processing. Recovered materials displace virgin material extraction, with the CO2 savings per tonne of recovered cobalt, for example, estimated at several tonnes of CO2 equivalent avoided. The 480 car-equivalent figure is based on average Indian passenger vehicle annual emissions estimates.
